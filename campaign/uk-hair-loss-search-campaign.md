# UK Early-Intervention Hair Loss Search Campaign

Research date: 9 August 2026  
Source: Google Ads Keyword Planner, Harley Street Medics account  
Market: United Kingdom · English · Google · July 2025–June 2026

## Strategic decision

### Big idea: **Not Bald. Not Too Early.**

Supporting campaign line: **Check the change before the gap.**

The audience does not identify as a transplant patient. They are noticing an early change—more hair in the shower, temples shifting, reduced crown density, a widening part or sudden diffuse shedding—and want to know whether it matters.

The campaign should not lead with PRP or exosomes for symptom-led traffic. It should lead with clarity:

> One clear photo can show whether the visible pattern may still be worth treating.

The free 60-second analysis is the offer. PRP and exosome therapy are possible next steps, not the opening claim.

## Core audience insight

The emotional problem is uncertainty, not baldness:

- “Is this normal shedding or the beginning of something?”
- “My hairline looks different, but I am not bald.”
- “My part looks wider; is it stress, hormones or actual hair loss?”
- “Do I need to act now, and is a transplant even relevant?”
- “Will a clinic sell me treatment even if it will not work?”

The answer offered by the product is evidence before commitment: a private, low-friction pre-assessment that can also say treatment is not suitable.

### Symptom-led creative territories

**Male:** “The hairline has changed. That does not mean a transplant. Check the stage first.”

**Female:** “A wider part or more shedding deserves clarity, not guesswork.”

**Stress:** “Shedding after stress can look alarming. Start by understanding the visible pattern.”

Ad copy must remain factual and must not promise to stop, reverse or cure hair loss.

## Landing-page message hierarchy

1. Not Bald. Not Too Early.
2. Check the change before the gap.
3. One photo · around 60 seconds · free · private.
4. Show what the report assesses: hairline, temples, density and visible miniaturisation.
5. Explain the honest filter: PRP and exosomes depend on active follicles.
6. Show clinic result, doctors, pricing and finance after the assessment proposition.
7. Keep transplant language out of the main treatment promise; use it only to clarify audience fit.

## Conversion and bidding safeguards

Primary conversions:

- Qualified analysis completed
- Consultation booked
- Qualified consultation form submitted
- Qualified phone call (for example, over 60 seconds)

Secondary observations:

- Analysis started
- Photo uploaded
- Report viewed

Do not optimise equally toward every upload. If qualified-analysis conversion tracking is reliable, use conversion-led bidding. If the campaign is data-poor, begin with controlled traffic acquisition and CPC limits, then move to conversion-led bidding after sufficient qualified outcomes.

Do not use health-condition remarketing or sensitive-personalisation audiences. Search intent should do the targeting; demographic and audience signals should remain observation-only unless policy and evidence support otherwise.

## Initial budget weighting

- 35% early male hairline/thinning
- 30% female thinning/shedding
- 10% stress/telogen test
- 25% PRP, exosome and clinic intent

Adjust by qualified analysis, booking and attended-consultation rates—not raw form volume.
